### PEST: Technological factors
Five technological factors, each rated as an opportunity or threat with its impact, using Harvard references. The main point is that the technology IIT needs for sustainability is already mature and affordable. The hard part is joining it up, not buying something new.

### SWOT: Opportunities
Five external opportunities, each linked to IIT's own resources with a short "why IIT is well placed" paragraph:
1. Sustainability rankings
2. Demand for green skills
3. New SLFRS S1/S2 sustainability disclosure rules
4. Clean energy technology alongside the new Ratmalana campus
5. National policy support

A shorter table version matches the style of the team's Threats table (navy header, colour-coded impact).

### McKinsey 7S: IIT as a whole
A gap analysis comparing IIT's current state with its 2030 goal of being a leading regional university with a sustainable campus.

| Gap size | Elements |
|---|---|
| Large | Structure, Systems |
| Medium | Strategy, Style, Staff, Skills |
| Small | Shared Values |

It comes with an IIT organisation chart. Parts of the chart (e.g. corporate functions) are assumed and marked with a star.

### Corporate structure analysis
An 8-row table rates each part of IIT's structure as a Strength, Mixed or Weakness. IIT has a functional structure with strong central control. The Sustainability Office owns sustainability but has no real authority, so I recommend a **cross-functional sustainability steering group**.

### Business recommendations: "Measure, Manage, Engage, Share"
Ten initiatives were scored on five weighted criteria:

| Criterion | Weight |
|---|---|
| Impact | 30% |
| Fit with analysis | 20% |
| Cost | 20% |
| Digital dependence | 15% |
| Speed | 15% |

Initiatives scoring **3.7 or above** made the final programme:

1. **Campus Sustainability Data Hub** (MVP)
2. IT Asset and E-waste Tracker
3. Smart Energy and Water at the Ratmalana campus
4. GreenIIT app and reuse marketplace
5. Open Reporting, Rankings and Living Lab

The steering group is the non-digital enabler behind all five. The rejected ideas were a commercial sustainability suite, an AI digital twin, blockchain tokens, an outreach platform and a transport app. The draft also includes a Value Proposition Canvas for the Data Hub and a swimlane process map of its monthly cycle.

**Benchmark check:** NSBM Green University's "8S model" is MTI Consulting's strategic planning method, not a business model, so it doesn't compete with the Data Hub idea. NSBM's published green practices don't include data monitoring. Monash University's Net Zero 2030 plan follows the same "measure first, then share" approach.

## Key findings

- The technology isn't the problem. Joining systems together and having good data is.
- IIT's strongest opportunities are rankings, green skills and the new Ratmalana campus.
- IIT's biggest internal gaps are Structure and Systems. Its values and people are strong.
- The Sustainability Office has responsibility but no authority, so it needs a steering group.
- IIT can't improve what it doesn't measure, which is why the Data Hub comes first.
