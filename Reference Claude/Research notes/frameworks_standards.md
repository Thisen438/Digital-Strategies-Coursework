## 1. Strategic choice frameworks: SAFe (suitability, acceptability, feasibility) and weighted scoring / multi-criteria decision analysis (MCDA)

### Takeaway
SAFe comes from *Exploring Strategy*. Pearson lists the 12th edition (published 16 December 2019, © 2020) with Richard Whittington as first author, and the criteria sit in Chapter 12, "Evaluating Strategies". The UK government's *Multi-criteria analysis: a manual* (DCLG, 2009) gives a citable, step-by-step weighted-scoring method (a "linear additive" model) that turns SAFe into a transparent option ranking.

- What the SAFe criteria mean, as consistently described in secondary study sources:
  - **Suitability** tests the strategic logic: does the strategy address the opportunities and constraints of the organisation's strategic position? SWOT is one way to assess it.
  - **Acceptability** asks whether the expected performance outcomes meet stakeholders' expectations. It covers **risk, returns and stakeholder reactions**. Suggested tools include stakeholder mapping, sensitivity analysis ("what if" testing of key assumptions), profitability measures, cost-benefit analysis, real options and shareholder value analysis.
  - **Feasibility** asks whether the organisation has the resources and capabilities to deliver the strategy, such as capital, people, time, market access and expertise.
  - The lower-case "e" in SAFe stands for evaluation.
  - Sources: [Saylor Academy, Making Strategy Effective](https://learn.saylor.org/mod/page/view.php?id=32138); [MindTools, Testing Strategic Options](https://www.mindtools.com/a83cedd/testing-strategic-options/); [aCOWtancy ACCA SBL notes, Assessing the options](https://www.acowtancy.com/textbook/acca-sbl/c5-strategic-choices/assessing-the-options/notes); [O'Reilly, Strategic Evaluation and Choice](https://oreilly.com/library/view/management-accounting-in/9781947843820/text/07_Chapter_7.xhtml). All secondary.
- Bibliographic details of the MCDA manual. Title page: *Multi-criteria analysis: a manual*, January 2009, Department for Communities and Local Government, London, © Crown copyright 2009, ISBN 978-1-4098-1023-0 — [GOV.UK PDF (primary)](https://assets.publishing.service.gov.uk/government/uploads/system/uploads/attachment_data/file/7612/1132618.pdf). LSE Research Online lists the authors as Dodgson, Spackman, Pearman and Phillips — [LSE Research Online](https://eprints.lse.ac.uk/12761/). The original manual of December 2000 was amended in January 2009 to update the software information. The manual complements monetary techniques such as cost-benefit analysis, because MCA "does not necessarily rely on monetary valuations" — [GOV.UK publication page](https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/multi-criteria-analysis-manual-for-making-government-policy)
- The manual's Figure 5.1, "Steps in a multi-criteria analysis" (primary):
  1. Establish the decision context.
  2. Identify the options.
  3. Identify the objectives and criteria.
  4. Describe the expected performance of each option against the criteria, and "score" the options.
  5. Assign weights for each criterion to reflect its relative importance.
  6. Combine the weights and scores to derive an overall value.
  7. Examine the results.
  8. Conduct a sensitivity analysis of the results to changes in scores or weights.
  — [GOV.UK PDF](https://assets.publishing.service.gov.uk/government/uploads/system/uploads/attachment_data/file/7612/1132618.pdf)
- The linear additive model, quoted from the manual (primary): an option's overall value is found "by multiplying the value score on each criterion by the weight of that criterion, and then adding all those weighted scores together". The manual adds that this "is only appropriate if the criteria are mutually preference independent". "Most MCA approaches use this additive model" — [GOV.UK PDF §4.7](https://assets.publishing.service.gov.uk/government/uploads/system/uploads/attachment_data/file/7612/1132618.pdf)

### Inferences
- Cite the book as Whittington et al. (2020) to match Pearson's author order. If the module guidance says "Johnson et al. (2020)", the report can note that this is the same 12th edition.
- A defensible method for the Business Recommendations section:
  - Use SAFe as the evaluation lens.
  - Make the three criteria explicit weighted criteria under DCLG steps 3 to 6. Example sub-criteria for IIT:
    - **Suitability:** does the option close the "no consolidated data" gap, and does it serve the GRI report and the 2028 GreenMetric/THE entry?
    - **Acceptability:** risk; return on the LKR 80m; reactions of students, estates, finance and the board.
    - **Feasibility:** one developer plus final-year students; the three-year budget; the new-campus construction timeline.
  - Then run step 8, a sensitivity test on the weights, to show the recommendation holds under different weightings.
- Under the linear additive model the criteria should be "preference independent". Avoid double counting: for example, do not score "cost" under both acceptability (returns) and feasibility (financial).

  - "Feasibility is concerned with whether a strategy could work in practice."

## 2. Information systems (IS) strategy frameworks: Ward and Peppard's application portfolio matrix, McFarlan's strategic grid, and Ward and Daniel's benefits dependency network (BDN)

### Takeaway
- **Application portfolio matrix.** Ward and Peppard's *Strategic Planning for Information Systems* is in its 3rd edition (Wiley, 2002). Its successor was retitled *The Strategic Management of Information Systems: Building a Digital Strategy* (Peppard and Ward, 4th edn, Wiley, 2016). The matrix's four segments are strategic, high potential, key operational and support.
- **McFarlan's strategic grid.** It has four quadrants (support, factory, turnaround, strategic) and is usually dated to McFarlan and McKenney (1983).
- **Benefits management.** The current edition of Ward and Daniel's *Benefits Management* is the 2nd (Wiley, 2012). The benefits dependency network comes from Cranfield's benefits management approach.

- McFarlan's strategic grid was proposed by F. Warren McFarlan and James L. McKenney in 1983. Its four quadrants are **support, factory, turnaround and strategic**:

### Inferences
- **Application portfolio for IIT** (my classification, based on the segment definitions):
  - Existing smart-meter vendor portals and utility billing records are **support**.
  - The consolidated energy, water and waste data platform and the GRI data pipeline are **key operational** once the institute depends on them for annual reporting and ranking submissions. They could be argued as **strategic** while they underpin differentiation in the 2028 ranking bid.
  - A student engagement app, or machine-learning forecasting built by final-year students, is **high potential**: prototype it, evaluate it and drop it if it fails.

  - **Business benefits:** measured reductions in electricity and water use; staff time saved on data compilation; GreenMetric points on the ICT, monitoring and dashboard indicators.

  - **Enabling changes:** data governance roles; metering standards; training; data-sharing with the landlord or utility.

## 3. Value Proposition Canvas (VPC), Minimum Viable Product (MVP) and BPMN 2.0

### Takeaway
- **Value Proposition Canvas:** a customer profile (jobs, pains, gains) is matched against a value map (products and services, pain relievers, gain creators) to reach "fit".
- **Minimum viable product:** Ries defines it as the version of a product that yields the most validated learning for the least effort.
- **BPMN:** I verified the core notation directly in OMG BPMN 2.0.2 (formal/2013-12-09). Note that ISO/IEC 19510:2013 is identical to **BPMN 2.0.1**, not 2.0.2.

### Cited Findings
- **Customer profile** of the Value Proposition Canvas:
  - Customer jobs, which can be functional, social or emotional.
  - Pains: risks, obstacles and bad outcomes.
  - Gains: desired outcomes.
- **Value map:** products and services, pain relievers and gain creators.
  - Pain relievers are the specific ways the offering removes or reduces a pain.
  - Gain creators are the specific ways it produces a gain.
- **Fit** is reached when pain relievers and gain creators match the most important jobs, pains and gains. Rank items by importance and judge fit against the top-ranked items.
- Sources: [Wade Institute, Value Proposition Canvas tool](https://studio.wadeinstitute.org.au/tool-detail-value-proposition-canvas.html); [Umbrex, Value Proposition Canvas](https://umbrex.com/resources/frameworks/organization-frameworks/value-proposition-canvas/); [FH Joanneum teaching notes](https://encore.fh-joanneum.at/wp-content/uploads/2022/09/6_Value-Proposition.pdf). All secondary; they attribute the canvas to *Value Proposition Design* (2014).
- Ries's definition: a minimum viable product is the "version of a new product which allows a team to collect the maximum amount of validated learning about customers with the least effort" — [Wikipedia, Lean startup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lean_startup); [Lean Startup Co., What is an MVP?](https://leanstartup.co/what-is-an-mvp/); [MIT Orbit, What is a Minimum Viable Product](https://orbit-kb.mit.edu/hc/en-us/articles/206440733-What-is-a-Minimum-Viable-Product-MVP)
- The MVP drives the **Build–Measure–Learn** feedback loop. Practitioner sources stress that an MVP is primarily a learning tool, not just a stripped-down first release — [FourWeekMBA, Minimum viable product](https://fourweekmba.com/minimum-viable-product/); [Lean Startup Co.](https://leanstartup.co/what-is-an-mvp/)
- **OMG BPMN 2.0.2** (primary): "Business Process Model and Notation (BPMN) Version 2.0.2", Date: December 2013, OMG Document Number **formal/2013-12-09**. "Version 2.0.2 contains a minor change to Clause 15" — [OMG BPMN 2.0.2 PDF](https://www.omg.org/spec/BPMN/2.0.2/PDF). OMG's version history lists the adoption of 2.0.2 as January 2014 — [OMG BPMN specification page](https://www.omg.org/spec/BPMN/)
- **ISO/IEC 19510:2013** ("Information technology — Object Management Group Business Process Model and Notation") is identical to OMG BPMN **2.0.1**. It defines the notation and semantics of Collaboration, Process and Choreography diagrams — [Standards Council of Canada standards database](https://scc-ccn.ca/standardsdb/standards/8154396); [SFS (Finnish Standards) store](https://store.sfs.fi/en/isoiec-19510-2013)
- BPMN's five basic categories of elements, quoted from clause 7 of the specification (primary):
  - **Flow Objects:** Events, Activities, Gateways.
  - **Data:** Data Objects, Data Inputs, Data Outputs, Data Stores.
  - **Connecting Objects:** Sequence Flows, Message Flows, Associations, Data Associations.
  - **Swimlanes:** Pools, Lanes.
  - **Artifacts:** Group, Text Annotation.
  — [OMG BPMN 2.0.2 PDF, §7.3](https://www.omg.org/spec/BPMN/2.0.2/PDF)
- Element definitions from Table 7.1, "Basic Modeling Elements" (primary, quoted or closely paraphrased):
  - **Event:** "something that 'happens' during the course of a Process ... There are three types of Events, based on when they affect the flow: Start, Intermediate, and End". Drawn as circles.
  - **Activity:** "a generic term for work that company performs ... The types of Activities that are a part of a Process Model are: Sub-Process and Task, which are rounded rectangles".
  - **Gateway:** "used to control the divergence and convergence of Sequence Flows ... it will determine branching, forking, merging, and joining of paths".
  - **Sequence Flow:** shows "the order that Activities will be performed".
  - **Message Flow:** shows "the flow of Messages between two Participants ... two separate Pools in a Collaboration Diagram will represent the two Participants".
  - **Pool:** "the graphical representation of a Participant in a Collaboration"; it may be a "black box".
  - **Lane:** "a sub-partition within a Process, sometimes within a Pool ... used to organize and categorize Activities".
  - **Data Object:** provides "information about what Activities require to be performed and/or what they produce".
  - **Message:** depicts "the contents of a communication between two Participants".
  - **Group:** groups elements in the same Category without affecting Sequence Flows.
  — [OMG BPMN 2.0.2 PDF, Table 7.1](https://www.omg.org/spec/BPMN/2.0.2/PDF)
- Gateway types in the specification: Exclusive, Inclusive, Parallel, Complex and Event-Based (§10.6.2–10.6.6) — [OMG BPMN 2.0.2 PDF](https://www.omg.org/spec/BPMN/2.0.2/PDF)

### Inferences
- **Value Proposition Canvas for IIT:** it suits three customer segments.
  - **Estates/facilities and finance.** Jobs: compile GRI and ranking data; cut utility bills. Pains: no consolidated data; manual spreadsheets; evidence chasing. Gains: automated kWh-per-person and carbon calculations.
  - **Students.** Jobs: see the impact of their actions; take part. Pains: invisible consumption. Gains: gamified feedback.
  - **Senior management.** Jobs: reputation, ranking entry, accreditation evidence.
- **MVP for IIT:** a scoped first release could be:
  - ingest existing smart-meter data for one building into a time-series store;
  - show a live dashboard;
  - auto-calculate GreenMetric EC4 (kWh per person) and EC8 (tCO2 per person).
  This is the cheapest test of whether the data is usable and whether staff use it, which is the "validated learning". It also directly lifts GreenMetric's ICT monitoring indicator EC11 from "planning" to "implemented".
- **BPMN for the "data-to-report" process:**
  - **Pools:** IIT, the utility/landlord, and the GreenMetric secretariat or THE.
  - **Lanes inside IIT:** Estates, Developer, Sustainability Officer, Finance, Management.
  - **Events:** a timer start event (monthly) and an end event (report published or submission made).
  - **Tasks:** collect meter data, validate, calculate, approve.
  - **Gateways:** an exclusive gateway for "data passes validation?".
  - **Data objects:** meter readings, evidence files, GRI content index.
  - **Message flows:** to the external pools, for bills and submissions.
- **BPMN citation:** cite OMG BPMN 2.0.2 for the notation, and cite ISO/IEC 19510:2013 as the international standard while noting that it equals 2.0.1. Do not write "ISO/IEC 19510:2013 (BPMN 2.0.2)".

## 4. Reference architecture for campus energy and water monitoring and sustainability data platforms

### Takeaway
A mature, largely open-source stack is available:
- **Data acquisition:** MQTT (an OASIS standard), BACnet (ASHRAE), LoRaWAN (ITU-T Y.4480) and Modbus.
- **Storage:** InfluxDB 3 Core (MIT/Apache 2.0, generally available since April 2025) or TimescaleDB (an Apache-2 edition, or a free but non-OSI "TSL" community edition).
- **Dashboards:** Grafana (AGPLv3 since 2021).
- **Energy-monitoring platforms:** OpenEnergyMonitor's emoncms (AGPL).
- **Semantic model:** Brick Schema (v1.4.x, actively maintained). ASHRAE 223P is still at public-review stage.

Licence differences (permissive versus AGPL versus source-available) matter for a student-built app.

### Cited Findings
**Data acquisition and communication protocols**

### Inferences
- **Suggested layered reference architecture for IIT.** This is my synthesis from the findings above, not a published standard:
  1. **Field layer.** Existing smart meters, typically Modbus RTU/TCP or a vendor cloud API. A BACnet/IP BMS on the new campus. LoRaWAN sensors for retrofitting water meters and waste-bin weights where cabling is costly.
  2. **Edge layer.** A gateway that polls Modbus/BACnet and publishes to an **MQTT** broker. Using MQTT decouples devices from applications and makes the new campus "plug-in".
  3. **Storage layer.** A time-series database: InfluxDB 3 Core (permissive MIT/Apache, so no copyleft risk) or TimescaleDB on PostgreSQL. TimescaleDB is attractive if the developer prefers SQL and relational joins to building metadata. Its Community (TSL) features are free for internal use, because IIT would not resell it as a database service.
  4. **Semantic layer.** A Brick model of sites, buildings, floors, meters and points. This lets the same queries roll up kWh per building, per m² and per person for GreenMetric and GRI. It also allows the new-campus BMS vendor to be contractually required to deliver Brick-compatible point metadata, aligned with the direction of ASHRAE 223P.
  5. **Analytics and presentation.** Grafana OSS for operational dashboards; this directly evidences the GreenMetric ICT indicators SI11, EC11, WS7, WR6, TR9 and ED14. Power BI or Excel can be used for board and finance reporting if IIT already licenses Microsoft 365.
  6. **API layer.** A small REST API serving aggregated, privacy-safe data to the student app.
  7. **Reporting layer.** Calculation scripts for the GHG inventory, GRI 102/103/303/306 and GreenMetric formulas.
- **Licence implications.** AGPL components (Grafana, emoncms) can be used freely internally. If students modify them and serve them over the network, the modified source must be published, so prefer configuration or plugins over forking. Permissive components (InfluxDB 3 Core, Apache-2 TimescaleDB) carry the fewest obligations.
- **Capacity fit.** One developer plus students argues for configuration over custom code: off-the-shelf broker, database and dashboard, with student projects focused on high-potential add-ons such as forecasting or the app. OpenEMS is aimed at energy *management and control* (e.g., batteries and PV). That is likely more than IIT needs in a monitoring-first phase, and this is a judgement rather than a verified fact.

## 5. Carbon and sustainability accounting: GHG Protocol, GRI topic standards, ISO 50001 and ISO 14001

### Takeaway
- **GHG Protocol.** The Corporate Standard (2004) and Scope 2 Guidance (2015) still apply. Scope 2 Guidance requires **dual reporting** (location-based and market-based) wherever contractual instruments exist. A Scope 2 revision is under way: consultation ran from October 2025 to January 2026, and the revised standard is expected in 2027.
- **GRI.** For any report published on or after 1 January 2027, **GRI 103: Energy 2025** and **GRI 102: Climate Change 2025** replace GRI 302 and GRI 305. GRI 303 (2018) and GRI 306 (2020) continue.
- **ISO 14001.** ISO 14001:2015 was superseded by **ISO 14001:2026** (published April 2026, with a 36-month transition).
- **ISO 50001.** ISO 50001:2018 remains the energy management standard, built on Plan-Do-Check-Act (PDCA), an energy review, an energy baseline (EnB) and energy performance indicators (EnPIs).

### Cited Findings
**GHG Protocol Corporate Standard and Scope 2 Guidance**
- The Scope 2 Guidance executive summary is subtitled "An amendment to the GHG Protocol Corporate Standard". It refers to "the publication of the Corporate Standard in 2004" — [GHG Protocol, Scope 2 Guidance executive summary (primary)](https://ghgprotocol.org/sites/default/files/2022-12/Scope2_ExecSum_Final.pdf)
- The Scope 2 Guidance (2015) was developed with input from over 200 representatives of companies, utilities, government, academia and civil society in more than 23 countries. The publisher is the GHG Protocol, a WRI and WBCSD partnership — [GHG Protocol, Scope 2 Guidance page](https://ghgprotocol.org/scope-2-guidance)
- The two methods, quoted from the executive summary (primary):
  - "A **location-based method** reflects the average emissions intensity of grids on which energy consumption occurs (using mostly grid-average emission factor data)."
  - "A **market-based method** reflects emissions from electricity that companies have purposefully chosen (or their lack of choice). It derives emission factors from contractual instruments..."
  - Contractual instruments "can include energy attribute certificates (RECs, GOs, etc.), direct contracts ..., supplier-specific emission rates, and other default emission factors representing the untracked or unclaimed energy and emissions (termed the residual mix)".
  — [Scope 2 Guidance executive summary](https://ghgprotocol.org/sites/default/files/2022-12/Scope2_ExecSum_Final.pdf)
- The dual-reporting rule, quoted from the executive summary (primary): "For companies with any operations in markets providing product or supplier-specific data in the form of contractual instruments, companies shall report scope 2 according to a location-based method and a market-based method ... Companies shall choose which method's results to use for goal setting and other benchmarks" — [Scope 2 Guidance executive summary](https://ghgprotocol.org/sites/default/files/2022-12/Scope2_ExecSum_Final.pdf)
- The **eight Scope 2 Quality Criteria** that contractual instruments must meet (primary, paraphrased from Table 1):
  1. Convey the direct GHG emission rate attribute.
  2. Be the only instruments carrying that claim.
  3. Be tracked and redeemed, retired or cancelled by or for the reporting entity.
  4. Be issued and redeemed as close as possible to the period of consumption.
  5. Be sourced from the same market as the reporting entity's operations.
  6. For utility-specific factors, be calculated on delivered electricity.
  7. For direct purchase or on-site generation, have all claims transferred to the reporting entity only.
  8. An adjusted residual mix must be available, or its absence disclosed.
  — [Scope 2 Guidance executive summary](https://ghgprotocol.org/sites/default/files/2022-12/Scope2_ExecSum_Final.pdf)
- **Revision status.**
  - The GHG Protocol held a public consultation on the Scope 2 Guidance from **20 October 2025 to 31 January 2026** — [GHG Protocol, Scope 2 Guidance page](https://ghgprotocol.org/scope-2-guidance)
  - The draft proposes requiring **hourly matching and deliverability** for market-based claims — [PwC Viewpoint, GHG Protocol announces Scope 2 Public Consultation](https://viewpoint.pwc.com/us/en/pwc/in-briefs/ib_int202521.html); [GHG Protocol blog, hourly matching and deliverability](https://ghgprotocol.org/blog/upcoming-scope-2-public-consultation-hourly-matching-and-deliverability). The package also includes an exemption for smaller organisations and a load-profile option for organisations without hourly data — [GHG Protocol blog, overview of revisions](https://ghgprotocol.org/blog/upcoming-scope-2-public-consultation-overview-revisions)
  - The GHG Protocol's milestone table, as reported by PwC, lists a 2026 draft for consultation on additional topics and a **revised Scope 2 Standard in 2027** — [PwC Viewpoint](https://viewpoint.pwc.com/us/en/pwc/in-briefs/ib_int202521.html)
  - Secondary commentary reports weak support for hourly matching and deliverability. Envolv cites about 1,100 responses — [Envolv](https://www.envolv.ch/en/insights/ghg-protocol-scope-2-consultation). OneStopESG says 70% of 909 respondents on hourly matching gave little or no support, and 59% opposed deliverability — [OneStopESG](https://onestopesg.com/esg-resources/article/ghg-protocol-scope-2-revision). Neither figure is confirmed by an official GHG Protocol publication.

**GRI topic standards**
- **GRI 103: Energy 2025** (primary): "EFFECTIVE DATE: 1 JANUARY 2027 ... This Standard is effective for reports or other materials published on or after 1 January 2027". Its disclosures are:
  - 103-1 Energy policies and commitments.
  - 103-2 Energy consumption and self-generation within the organization.
  - 103-3 Upstream and downstream energy consumption.
  - 103-4 Energy intensity.
  - 103-5 Reduction in energy consumption.
  The standard cross-refers to GRI 101: Biodiversity 2024 and to Disclosures 102-5, 102-6 and 102-7 in GRI 102: Climate Change 2025 — [GRI 103: Energy 2025 PDF](https://www.globalreporting.org/pdf.ashx?id=29537)
- GRI 102: Climate Change 2025 and GRI 103: Energy 2025 were published in **June 2025**. They replace **GRI 305: Emissions 2016** and **GRI 302: Energy 2016** respectively; GRI 102 also replaces Disclosure 201-2 on climate-related financial implications. Early adoption is permitted or encouraged — [Mondaq, GRI releases new climate and energy standards](https://www.mondaq.com/unitedstates/climate-change/1656826/gri-releases-new-climate-and-energy-standards-significant-reporting-changes-ahead); [BDO Switzerland, GRI updates 2025–2027](https://www.bdo.ch/en-gb/insights/gri-updates-2025-2027-transparency-and-impact-in-sustainability); [Jones Day, Trending Now in ESG](https://trendingnowinesg.jonesday.com/post/102kqs7/gri-releases-new-climate-and-energy-standards-significant-reporting-changes-ahe).   - **GRI 103:** total energy consumption split by renewable and non-renewable sources, and by internal and external use.
  — [Mondaq](https://www.mondaq.com/unitedstates/climate-change/1656826/gri-releases-new-climate-and-energy-standards-significant-reporting-changes-ahead); [BDO Switzerland](https://www.bdo.ch/en-gb/insights/gri-updates-2025-2027-transparency-and-impact-in-sustainability)
- **GRI 303: Water and Effluents 2018** (303-3 to 303-5) and **GRI 306: Waste 2020** (306-3 to 306-5) disclosures are listed together in corporate GRI content indexes — [Sodexo Universal Registration Document 2024](https://sodexo.publispeak.com/universal-registration-document-fiscal-2024/article/325/); [Metsä Board Annual Review 2023](https://metsaboardannualreport.metsagroup.com/view/230548215/182/). **ISO 50001 energy management systems**
- ISO 50001:2018 is built on **Plan-Do-Check-Act**. The planning stage covers the energy review, identifying **significant energy uses (SEUs)**, and setting **EnPIs**, the **energy baseline (EnB)** and objectives — [energy-data.io, ISO 50001](https://energy-data.io/standards/iso-50001/). **ISO 14001 environmental management systems**

  - It follows the Harmonized Structure.
  - Its themes include climate-related considerations, environmental performance, life-cycle thinking and structured change management.

  - A **36-month transition period** applies.

### Inferences
- **IIT's first GRI-aligned annual report will fall under the new standards.** It will be published in 2027 or 2028, so it should be built against **GRI 103 (energy)** and **GRI 102 (climate)** rather than GRI 302 and 305, plus GRI 303 (water) and GRI 306 (waste). A report or data model designed today around "GRI 302/305" will be out of date on day one.
- **Data the platform must capture to satisfy these standards:**
  - **Energy:** energy consumption and self-generation by source, including rooftop solar if installed; renewable versus non-renewable; intensity denominators (per student/staff full-time equivalent, per m²).
  - **Scope 1 emissions:** diesel generators, LPG, vehicle fuel, refrigerant top-ups.
  - **Scope 2 emissions:** location-based, using a Sri Lankan grid factor; market-based if IIT buys certificates such as I-RECs.
  - **Water:** withdrawal by source (municipal supply, groundwater, rainwater harvesting), discharge and consumption.
  - **Waste:** waste generated, diverted from disposal and directed to disposal.
- **Two carbon calculations from one activity dataset:**
  - GreenMetric's simplified method: a fixed 0.84 tCO2/MWh electricity coefficient plus fixed transport coefficients (Section 6).
  - The GHG Protocol / GRI 102 inventory, using appropriate grid and fuel emission factors.
  The platform should store activity data once and compute both, with the method documented so the two figures are not confused.
- **ISO 50001's PDCA maps onto the platform:**
  - **Plan:** an energy review from smart-meter data; set the EnB, for example 12 months of normal operation; choose EnPIs such as kWh per person and kWh/m², normalised for cooling degree-days or occupancy if those variables are significant.
  - **Do / Check:** dashboards and monthly review.
  - **Act:** corrective actions.
  The new campus needs its own baseline once operation stabilises. Given the budget, **align with** ISO 50001 and ISO 14001:2026 rather than seeking certification in the first three years; this is a judgement.

**ICT indicators** (SI11, EC11, WS7, WR6, TR9, ED14)
- Each asks for "planning, implementation, monitoring and/or evaluation of all programs ... through the utilization of ICT". GreenMetric states "We recommend dashboard to input EC data" (similar wording for waste and water).
- The answer scale is: [1] None; [2] planning stage; [3] implemented; [4] implemented and evaluated; [5] "implemented, evaluated, and is currently revised". Evidence is required.

**EC2 smart building**
- A smart building must have automation, safety, energy, water, indoor environment and lighting features, supported by a BMS, BIM, BAS or FMS, with at least five requirements.
- Score = smart-building floor area / total building floor area (2.2/1.7 × 100%), banded: [1] <1%; [2] 1–25%; [3] >25–50%; [4] >50–75%; [5] >75%.

**EC4 electricity per person**
- Question 2.6 asks for "the total energy used in the last 12 months in your entire university area (in kilowatt hours or kWh) for all purposes such as lighting, heating, cooling, running university laboratories".
- EC4 = (2.6) / (1.12 + 1.14), banded: [1] ≥2,400 kWh; [2] >1,500–2,400; [3] >600–1,500; [4] ≥250–600; [5] <250 kWh per person.
- Population: question 1.12 asks for "regular students (full-time and part-time) ... (Effective Full-Time Students (EFTS))", excluding short-term students. Question 1.14 asks for "effective full-time academic staff ... and administrative staff". Online students (1.13) are counted separately.

**EC8 carbon footprint per person**
- Question 2.11 asks for the total carbon footprint over the last 12 months, excluding "flights and secondary carbon sources, such as dishes and clothes".
- EC8 = (2.11) / (1.12 + 1.14), banded: [1] ≥2.05 t; [2] >1.11–2.05; [3] >0.42–1.11; [4] >0.10–0.42; [5] <0.10 t per person.

**Appendix 4, carbon footprint method**
- The method is the sum of electricity and transport emissions, "based on ... www.carbonfootprint.com". Electricity: "(electricity usage per year in kWh/1000) x 0.84", where 0.84 is "the coefficient to convert kWh to metric tons".
- Transport covers on-campus distances only, over 240 working days:
  - Shuttle: buses × trips per day × km per day × 240/100 × 0.01.
  - Cars: cars × 2 × km × 240/100 × 0.02.
  - Motorcycles: motorcycles × 2 × km × 240/100 × 0.01.

## 7. Security and privacy standards for IoT/OT and student apps

### Takeaway
- **NIST SP 800-82 Rev. 3** (September 2023) explicitly covers building automation systems and recommends:
  - defence-in-depth;
  - segmentation and isolation of IT and OT networks, with a DMZ;
  - multi-factor authentication (MFA) for remote access;
  - disabling unused ports.
  A Rev. 4 draft was out for comment in late 2026.
- **ETSI EN 303 645 V3.1.3** (2024-09) gives 13 baseline provisions usable as an IoT procurement checklist.
- **IEC 62443** adds zones, conduits and security levels.
- **Privacy by Design** (Cavoukian, 2009, revised 2011) gives seven principles for the student app.
- **Sri Lanka's PDPA** main obligations are reported to take effect on 1 January 2027.

### Cited Findings
- **NIST SP 800-82 Rev. 3** (primary):
  - Title: *Guide to Operational Technology (OT) Security*, NIST SP 800-82 Rev. 3, final, 28 September 2023, DOI 10.6028/NIST.SP.800-82r3.
  - Authors: Keith Stouffer, Michael Pease, CheeYee Tang, Timothy Zimmerman, Victoria Pillitteri and Suzanne Lightman (NIST), with Adam Hahn, Stephanie Saravia, Aslam Sherule and Michael Thompson (MITRE).
  - It supersedes Rev. 2 (June 2015). Its scope examples include "industrial control systems, building automation systems, transportation systems, physical access control systems".
  - A planning note dated 21 September 2026 points to an **initial public draft of Rev. 4**, with comments due 30 November 2026.
  — [NIST CSRC, SP 800-82 Rev. 3](https://csrc.nist.gov/pubs/sp/800/82/r3/final)
- **NIST's defence-in-depth guidance** (primary, executive summary): defence in depth means "layering security mechanisms such that the impact of a failure in any one mechanism is minimized ... Organizations should not rely on 'security by obscurity.'" A typical OT defence-in-depth strategy includes:
  - security policies and training specific to the OT system;
  - addressing security across the life cycle, from "architecture design, procurement, installation, operations, maintenance, and decommissioning";
  - "a network topology for the OT system that has multiple layers";
  - "logical separation between the corporate and OT networks (e.g., stateful inspection firewalls ..., unidirectional gateways)";
  - "a DMZ network architecture (e.g., prevent direct traffic between the corporate and OT networks)";
  - "multi-factor authentication for remote access";
  - redundancy;
  - graceful degradation;
  - "disabling unused ports and services on OT devices".
  — [NIST SP 800-82r3 PDF](https://nvlpubs.nist.gov/nistpubs/SpecialPublications/NIST.SP.800-82r3.pdf)
- **NIST on network segmentation** (primary, §5.2.3):
  - Network security covers "segmentation and isolation, centralizing logging, network monitoring, and malicious code protection", plus zero trust architecture.
  - §5.2.3.1: "A good practice for network architectures is to characterize, segment, and isolate IT and OT devices", for example by "management authority, level of trust, functional criticality, data flow, location". Organisations may use "the Purdue model, ISA-95 levels, the three-tier IIoT system architecture" and may incorporate "a DMZ as an enforcement boundary between network segments".
  - Appendix E.1 covers "Network Segmentation and Isolation".
  — [NIST SP 800-82r3 PDF](https://nvlpubs.nist.gov/nistpubs/SpecialPublications/NIST.SP.800-82r3.pdf)
- **ETSI EN 303 645 V3.1.3 (2024-09)**, *CYBER; Cyber Security for Consumer Internet of Things: Baseline Requirements*. Scope: "specifies high-level security and data protection provisions for consumer IoT devices". Clause 6 covers data protection provisions. Clause 5 provisions:
  - 5.1 No universal default passwords
  - 5.2 Implement a means to manage reports of vulnerabilities
  - 5.3 Keep software updated
  - 5.4 Securely store sensitive security parameters
  - 5.5 Communicate securely
  - 5.6 Minimize exposed attack surfaces
  - 5.7 Ensure software integrity
  - 5.8 Ensure that personal data is secure
  - 5.9 Make systems resilient to outages
  - 5.10 Examine system telemetry data
  - 5.11 Make it easy for users to delete user data
  - 5.12 Make installation and maintenance of devices easy
  - 5.13 Validate input data
  — [iTeh Standards catalogue, SIST EN 303 645 V3.1.3:2024](https://standards.iteh.ai/catalog/standards/sist/a9cbdb8b-3e14-4178-af7a-ded023baefe1/sist-en-303-645-v3-1-3-2024)
- **Clause 5.1 sub-provisions** require:
  - unique per-device or user-set passwords (5.1-1);
  - sufficiently randomised pre-installed passwords (5.1-2);
  - best-practice cryptography for authentication (5.1-3);
  - a simple way to change authentication values (5.1-4);
  - impracticable brute-force attacks over network interfaces (5.1-5).
  — [Zephyr Project documentation, ETSI 303 645](https://docs.zephyrproject.org/latest/security/standards/etsi-303645.html). The UK's Product Security and Telecommunications Infrastructure (PSTI) regime references provisions 5.1-1 and 5.1-2 for deemed compliance — [legislation.gov.uk, PSTI regulations 2023 schedule](https://legislation.gov.uk/ukdsi/2023/9780348249767/schedules/data.xht)
- **ISA/IEC 62443** (secondary):
  - It defines **zones** (groupings of assets sharing security requirements) and **conduits** (controlled communication paths between zones), with target **security levels** assigned to each zone and conduit.
  - Asset owners build a security programme under IEC 62443-2-1, partition systems into zones and conduits and assign target security levels under **IEC 62443-3-2**, and specify system requirements under IEC 62443-3-3.
  - The series covers building automation among other sectors. Sources disagree on the security-level range (SL 0–4 per IEC 62443-1-1 versus SL 1–4).
  — [Trout Software, IEC 62443](https://www.trout.software/resources/iec-62443); [CMS Conferences paper](https://openaccess-api.cms-conferences.org/articles/download/978-1-958651-90-2_34); [L-com, IEC 62443 guide](https://www.l-com.com/resources/blog/iec-62443-manufacturing-guide)
- **Privacy by Design:** Cavoukian, A., *Privacy by Design: The 7 Foundational Principles*, Information and Privacy Commissioner of Ontario, originally published August 2009 and revised January 2011. Verified principles include:
  - 1, Proactive not reactive: "comes before-the-fact, not after";
  - 2, Privacy as the default: where the need for personal information is unclear, "the default settings shall be the most privacy protective";
  - 3, Privacy embedded into design: "embedded into the design and architecture of IT systems and business practices";
  - 4, a positive-sum approach rather than trade-offs;
  - 7, "Respect for User Privacy — Keep it User-Centric".
  — [IPC Ontario PDF](https://ipc.on.ca/en/media/1236/download); [IETF slides, PbD seven foundational principles](https://www.ietf.org/slides/slides-privacyws-privacy-by-design-the-seven-foundational-principles-00.pdf). PbD was incorporated into the EU GDPR in 2018 — [HillNotes (Library of Parliament, Canada)](https://hillnotes.ca/2021/12/09/privacy-by-design-origin-and-purpose/)
- **Sri Lanka's Personal Data Protection Act (PDPA) No. 9 of 2022:**
  - Sri Lankan legal commentary reports that Extraordinary Gazette No. 2498/16 of 22 July 2026 sets **1 January 2027** as the operative date for sections 2 and 3 and Parts I and III of the Act.
  - Part VII, the administrative penalties, has not yet been brought into operation.
  - An earlier March 2025 date was withdrawn, and an amendment bill was tabled in March 2025.
  - The Data Protection Authority is operational. Sources differ on its start date: in force from 17 July 2023, or established August 2023.
  — [Neelakandan & Neelakandan, Data protection in transition](https://neelakandan.lk/insights/data-protection-in-transition-understanding-sri-lankas-new-legal-framework); [F J & G de Saram, Amendment Bill](https://www.fjgdesaram.com/news-insights/dissecting-the-provisions-of-the-amendment-bill-amending-the-personal-data-protection-act); [Hashtag Coders, Sri Lanka Data Protection Law 2026](https://hashtagcoders.lk/blogs/data-privacy-laws-sri-lanka-2026); [Data Protection Authority of Sri Lanka](https://www.dpa.gov.lk/)

### Inferences
- **Segmentation pattern for IIT, from NIST's guidance:**
  - Put smart meters, BMS controllers and gateways in an **OT zone** (VLAN or firewall segment) with no direct route from student Wi-Fi or the corporate LAN.
  - Have data leave the zone only through a **conduit**, for example a DMZ-hosted MQTT broker or one-way data push to the analytics zone.
  - Enforce MFA for any vendor remote access; disable unused ports and services; centralise logs.
  - Write these into the **new-campus BMS contract** using IEC 62443-3-2 language: zones and conduits, plus a vendor-declared target security level.
- **ETSI EN 303 645 as a procurement checklist.** Use its 13 provisions for any IoT sensor or gateway IIT buys, e.g., LoRaWAN water meters: no universal default passwords, a vulnerability disclosure route, a software update policy and secure communications. ETSI targets consumer IoT, but it is a widely referenced low-cost baseline. That is an inference; for enterprise-grade OT, IEC 62443-4-2 component requirements would be stricter.
- **Student app and Privacy by Design.** Default to aggregated building-level data; collect the minimum personal data; make participation opt-in; build in deletion; publish a privacy notice. If IIT's PDPA obligations start on 1 January 2027, the app's launch will likely fall under the Act, so complete a data protection impact assessment (DPIA) before launch, as good practice.

## 8. Higher-education guidance on digital and IT sustainability (Jisc, EDUCAUSE, EAUC)

### Takeaway
- **Jisc** (May 2024) found that staff and student IT use is the top perceived barrier to digital sustainability in tertiary education. It recommends lifecycle-based IT policies and efficient data storage.
- **EAUC's Standardised Carbon Emissions Framework (SCEF)** gives UK and Irish post-16 education a GHG Protocol-based, sector-standard way to report carbon. Version 2 adds guidance on digital and generative-AI emissions.
- I found no EDUCAUSE material in this session.

### Cited Findings
- **Jisc's May 2024 report:**
  - Title: *Digital sustainability in tertiary education: trends, challenges and sector insights*, based on a survey of almost 100 members.
  - Respondents named staff and student use of IT as the top perceived challenge. The top action being taken is sustainable policies for key stages of the IT product lifecycle.
  - In higher education, 45% of respondents already had net zero targets for 2035 or earlier.
  - The report sets out eight common digital sustainability needs and a roadmap for senior leaders.
  — [UKAuthority, Jisc identifies eight needs for digital sustainability in tertiary education](https://www.ukauthority.com/articles/jisc-identifies-eight-needs-for-digital-sustainability-in-tertiary-education/); [FE News, Jisc: the biggest challenge to digital sustainability is IT use](https://fenews.co.uk/?p=438179)
- **Practical steps highlighted by Jisc commentary:**
  - evaluating energy consumption;
  - managing hardware lifecycles;
  - improving software efficiency;
  - sustainable data storage.
  Examples of avoidable emissions include "saving unnecessary content to the cloud, leaving devices on standby, and sharing substantial amounts of data" — [FE News, Let's make digital sustainability in education a norm](https://fenews.co.uk/?p=440360); [FE News](https://fenews.co.uk/?p=438179)
- **EAUC's SCEF:**
  - It provides "post-16 education organisations with a consistent, sector-backed approach to calculating and reporting carbon emissions" in the UK and Ireland.
  - It is built on the GHG Protocol, framed for the education setting.
  - Version 2, on an EAUC resource page dated 1 September 2026, expands guidance on Scope 3, digital emissions, electricity and renewable energy reporting, and says where to report emissions from generative AI use.
  - The original was funded by the Department for Education under its Sustainability and Climate Change Strategy.
  - HESA's Estates Management Record is being revised to use SCEF carbon factors, with AUDE leading updates to non-carbon elements.
  - Colleges can choose from three reporting levels by data maturity.
  — [EAUC, SCEF](https://www.eauc.org.uk/scef); [EAUC, SCEF update](https://www.eauc.org.uk/standardised_carbon_emissions_framework_update); [EAUC, Flagship report accelerates tertiary education sector's path to net zero](https://eauc.org.uk/flagship_report_accelerates_tertiary_education_); [Wonkhe, Accelerating the sector's journey to net zero](https://wonkhe.com/blogs/accelerating-the-sectors-journey-to-net-zero/)
- **AASHE STARS** is one of the systems UI GreenMetric referred to when it was designed, which signals that it is a recognised HE sustainability rating framework — [UI GreenMetric Guideline 2025](https://uigreenmetric.com/wp-content/uploads/2025/10/UI-GreenMetric-Guideline-2025.pdf)

### Inferences
- **SCEF as a ready-made model.** SCEF is UK/Ireland-specific, but it is a ready-made education-sector interpretation of the GHG Protocol: boundaries and categories, plus explicit treatment of digital and AI emissions. IIT can cite it to justify its emissions boundary and categories in the GRI 102 inventory, and to include the footprint of its own digital estate. SCEF's three data-maturity levels are a useful model for a phased digital strategy:
  - Level 1: invoices and bills.
  - Level 2: meter data.
  - Level 3: sub-metered building-level data.
- **Jisc's warning about the platform itself.** Jisc's emphasis on device lifecycle and data-storage efficiency suggests the strategy should address the footprint of the platform it builds. Practical measures: data retention and downsampling rules for high-frequency meter data, and efficient hosting.
