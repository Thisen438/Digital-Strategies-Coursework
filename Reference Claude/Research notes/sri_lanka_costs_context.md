## Q1. Commercial rooftop solar PV: installed cost per kW, Colombo-area yield, battery storage cost, and current feed-in (Net Plus) tariffs

### Takeaway
PUCSL's own August 2026 tariff model benchmarks rooftop PV capex at **LKR 131,800/kW for 40-250 kW** (≈US$397/kW) and LKR 120,960/kW above 250 kW, with a 17% plant factor (≈1,489 kWh/kW/yr). PVGIS gives **≈1,369 kWh/kWp/yr for Ratmalana**. New prosumer tariffs from **25 Aug 2026** are LKR 23.11 / 19.15 / 17.11 / 15.81 per kWh by size band, which confirms the brief's figures. The biggest swing factor for any campus solar business case is regulatory. The Ministry/EDL reading is "Net Plus only for new connections", which pays about LKR 17/kWh. PUCSL insists net metering and net accounting continue, and those offset the GP-2 day rate of about LKR 51/kWh. This dispute was unresolved as of Sep-Nov 2026.

### Inferences
- [DERIVED] A 100 kWp campus roof yields ≈137-149 MWh/yr. The low end is PVGIS and the high end is the PUCSL plant factor. At the PUCSL 40-250 kW benchmark it costs ≈LKR 13.2m (≈US$39,700), about 16.5% of the LKR 80m envelope if solar capex were in scope. The brief mentions only "solar monitoring", so PV capex may sit outside the programme.
- [DERIVED] Value of each kWp per year:
  - If self-consumed or net-metered against the GP-2 day energy charge (LKR 51/kWh from 11 May 2026; see Q2): ≈LKR 69,800-75,900/kWp/yr.
  - If exported under Net Plus at LKR 17.11: ≈LKR 23,400-25,500/kWp/yr.
  - After 2% O&M (≈LKR 2,636/kW/yr), simple payback on LKR 131,800/kW is **≈1.8-2.0 years under net metering/self-consumption versus ≈5.8-6.3 years under Net Plus**. These figures ignore degradation, financing, any demand-charge effect and future tariff changes.
  - The dispute in the Cited Findings therefore roughly triples or cuts to a third the value of a campus PV array.
- [DERIVED] Solar output falls entirely within the GP-2 "Day" window (05:30-18:30). It does not offset the LKR 78/kWh peak (18:30-22:30) unless storage is added. Under current rules the BESS feed-in premium (LKR 45.53) applies only to systems above 250 kW. For a sub-250 kW campus system, BESS value would come from shifting self-consumption into the evening peak, which saves about LKR 27-38/kWh.
- [ASSUMPTION] For budgeting a 40-250 kW campus array, use LKR 130,000-150,000/kW. That spans the PUCSL 40-250 kW and 10-40 kW benchmarks. Add 10-15% contingency for structural/roof works and monitoring hardware on a new building.
- [DERIVED] Because PUCSL priced at 341.09 LKR/USD, its LKR benchmarks are ≈3% higher in USD at today's ~332.

## Q2. Electricity tariffs (GP-2 / GP-3) after the April and 11 May 2026 revisions, any Q4 2026 revision, and NWSDB water tariffs for institutional/commercial users

### Takeaway
A private institute falls under PUCSL's **General Purpose** tariff, which covers "educational establishments". From **11 May 2026**:
- **GP-2** (400/230 V, contract demand >42 kVA) pays **LKR 78 / 51 / 40 per kWh** (peak / day / off-peak), **LKR 1,800 per kVA** maximum demand and **LKR 6,000/month** fixed.
- **GP-3** (11 kV and above) pays **77 / 49 / 39**, **LKR 1,700/kVA** and LKR 6,000/month.

That is an 18% increase on the April 2026 rates. Q3 2026 brought no revision. For Q4 2026, PUCSL has proposed **+1.46%** after NSO/EDL sought **+2.88%**; the decision was still pending at 5 Nov 2026. NWSDB charges commercial premises a flat **LKR 150/m³** plus a stepped service charge of LKR 500-130,000/month, under Gazette 2398/19 of 21 Aug 2024.

## Q3. Latest official Sri Lanka grid emission factor for Scope 2 (location-based) and its publisher

### Takeaway
The national reference is the **Sri Lanka Sustainable Energy Authority (SLSEA)**'s annual *Sri Lanka Energy Balance*. The latest edition, **Energy Balance 2023** (file dated ~Aug 2026), gives:
- an **average grid emission factor of 0.4242 kgCO2/kWh for 2023** (0.4173 for 2022), the appropriate basis for location-based Scope 2;
- CDM-style margins for 2023 of operating margin 0.7049, build margin 0.6182 and combined margin 0.6616 kgCO2/kWh. These suit carbon-credit baselines, not inventories.

### Cited Findings
- [SOURCE] SLSEA Energy Balance 2023: "The Grid Emission Factors calculated for 2023 gives the Simple Operating Margin as **0.7049** kg-CO2/kWh, the Build Margin as **0.6182** kg-CO2/kWh and the Combined Margin as **0.6616** kg-CO2/kWh". Key statistics table: average emission factor **0.4173 (2022)** and **0.4242 (2023)** kg-CO2/kWh. — [SLSEA, Sri Lanka Energy Balance 2023](https://energy.gov.lk/images/energy-balance/energy-balance-2023.pdf)
  - **Internal inconsistency:** the extracted text also says "The Average Emission Factor for 2022 was estimated to be 0.4242". The table, and the secondary source below, attribute 0.4242 to 2023.
- [SOURCE] Energy Balance 2022 gives a 2022 simple operating margin of 0.7123 and a build margin of 0.5841 kg-CO2/kWh. — [SLSEA, Energy Balance 2022](https://www.energy.gov.lk/images/energy-balance/energy-balance-2022.pdf)

### Inferences
- [ASSUMPTION, method choice] Use **0.4242 kgCO2/kWh (SLSEA, 2023 vintage)** for location-based Scope 2 in IIT's GRI 305-2 disclosure. Disclose the vintage and source, and update when SLSEA publishes Energy Balance 2024. Using the combined margin (0.6616) instead would overstate Scope 2 by about 56%.
- [DERIVED] Each 100 MWh of campus consumption ≈ 42.4 tCO2. A 100 kWp array (137-149 MWh/yr) avoids ≈58-63 tCO2/yr at the average factor.
- [Inference] The factor will move with hydrology. PUCSL data show major-hydro output of 812 GWh in Q2 2023 against 1,704 GWh in Q2 2025, so the 2024-25 factors may come in lower. ### Gaps

## Q9. Personal Data Protection Act No. 9 of 2022 (as amended by Act No. 22 of 2025): obligations from 1 January 2027 (Gazette 2498/16)

### Takeaway
Gazette Extraordinary **No. 2498/16 (22 Jul 2026)** appoints **1 January 2027** for Sections 2-3, **Part I** (processing obligations) and **Part III** (controllers and processors). That brings in force:
- the **s12 Data Protection Management Programme**;
- **s20 DPOs** for specified controllers;
- **s24 DPIAs** for systematic or extensive evaluation including profiling;
- the **amended s26** on cross-border transfers.

**Part II (data-subject rights) and Part VII (penalties) await a further order.** Hosting student data in India-based cloud regions will need DPA-specified safeguards or another lawful ground.

### Cited Findings
- [SOURCE] Gazette 2498/16 of 22 Jul 2026 "appoints 1 January 2027 as the operational date for Sections 2 and 3, Part I on processing personal data, and Part III on controllers and processors". — [Ceylon Public Affairs](https://ceylonpublicaffairs.com/sri-lanka-pdpa-january-2027/)
- [SOURCE] The gazette is listed by the Data Protection Authority ("22/07/2026 – Extraordinary Gazette No. 2498/16"). — [DPA Sri Lanka, Guidelines and Gazettes](https://www.dpa.gov.lk/guidelines.php)
- [SOURCE] "Data subject rights under Part II and the penalty regime under Part VII wait on a further order." — [Concentric AI, 20 Aug 2026](https://concentric.ai/sri-lankas-pdpa-takes-effect-january-1-2027-what-global-enterprises-need-to-know/)
- [SOURCE] Section 12 (Part I) requires every controller to implement a Data Protection Management Programme. This is a documented system of internal controls that keeps catalogued records showing how each obligation is met, sits inside governance, sets up internal oversight and identifies breaches. — [Concentric AI](https://concentric.ai/sri-lankas-pdpa-takes-effect-january-1-2027-what-global-enterprises-need-to-know/)
- [SOURCE] Section 20 mandates DPOs for ministries and government departments, and for organisations whose core activities involve "regular and systematic monitoring, special category data at scale, or processing that risks harm to data subjects". — [Concentric AI](https://concentric.ai/sri-lankas-pdpa-takes-effect-january-1-2027-what-global-enterprises-need-to-know/)
- [SOURCE] Section 24 requires a personal-data-protection impact assessment before processing that involves "any systematic and extensive evaluation of personal data, including profiling". Section 24(4) requires re-evaluation when methodology, technology or process changes. The amended s24(5) lets the Authority call in assessments. — [Concentric AI](https://concentric.ai/sri-lankas-pdpa-takes-effect-january-1-2027-what-global-enterprises-need-to-know/)
- [SOURCE] Amendment Act No. 22 of 2025 "replaced Section 26 outright":
  - "The adequacy decision mechanism from the 2022 text is gone."
  - A controller or processor may transfer personal data abroad "only where it complies with Part I, Part II, and Sections 20 through 25, and adopts the safeguard instruments specified by an Authority directive".
  - "Explicit informed consent, contractual necessity, legal claims, public interest, and emergencies remain available as separate grounds."
  - — [Concentric AI](https://concentric.ai/sri-lankas-pdpa-takes-effect-january-1-2027-what-global-enterprises-need-to-know/) (vendor blog; the primary Act text link on documents.gov.lk returned 404)
- [SOURCE] The DPA consulted in late 2024 on a "Draft Directive on Specification of Instruments for Processing Personal Data Outside Sri Lanka". It covers Binding Corporate Rules, legal agreements imposing "binding and enforceable" obligations, codes of conduct and certification. It also adds a **Cross-Border Processing Impact Assessment** and, for some scenarios, a board resolution. The brief also records that the original 18 Mar 2025 date for Part III was repealed by Gazette 2427/34. — [Future of Privacy Forum, APAC cross-border transfers brief (Jul 2026)](https://fpf.org/wp-content/uploads/2026/07/FPF-Issue-Brief-Navigating-Cross-Border-Data-Transfers-in-the-APAC-Region-2023-2026.pdf); [DataGuidance, 16 Oct 2024](https://www.dataguidance.com/jurisdictions/sri-lanka)
- [SOURCE] Other features:
  - Penalties of up to **LKR 10 million per violation** (≈US$30,000), and directors' personal liability under s38(6) (both Part VII, not yet in force).
  - A "child" is anyone **under 16**, and children's data is special-category data.
  - Extraterritorial reach (s2).
  - The 2025 amendment gives one month to answer data-subject requests, extendable by two months.
  - — [Concentric AI](https://concentric.ai/sri-lankas-pdpa-takes-effect-january-1-2027-what-global-enterprises-need-to-know/)

### Inferences
- [Inference] As controller for the sustainability platform, the asset tracker (staff and student custodian names) and the student engagement app, IIT needs a s12 DPMP **from 1 Jan 2027**. That means a record of processing, governance and oversight, and breach identification.
  - If the engagement app scores or ranks students' behaviour (gamified leaderboards, location or room check-ins), that is "profiling". It points to a s24 DPIA before launch, and arguably to a DPO under s20 (regular and systematic monitoring).
  - Minimising personal data in the app (pseudonymous IDs, aggregate dashboards) reduces scope.
- [Inference] Hosting in Azure Central India or AWS Mumbai counts as processing outside Sri Lanka. Until the DPA's final directive is issued, rely on processor contracts with binding data-protection terms, which the draft directive anticipates, and/or explicit consent. Prepare a cross-border processing impact assessment. Meter, energy and water data with no personal identifiers falls outside the PDPA.
- [ASSUMPTION] Budget ≈LKR 1-2m over 3 years for legal review, DPIA/CBPIA work and part-time or outsourced DPO support. This is not sourced.

### Gaps
- The final DPA directive on cross-border instruments was not found; its status after the July 2026 gazette is unknown.
- The verbatim text of Amendment Act No. 22 of 2025 could not be retrieved (documents.gov.lk returned 404). The s26 wording relies on a vendor summary.
- No DPA rules were found on DPO qualifications, controller registration or fees.
- How s26's reference to Part II works while Part II is not yet in force is unclear.
