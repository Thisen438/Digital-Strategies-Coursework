# Measured impact of digitally enabled campus sustainability initiatives (evidence base for the IIT Sri Lanka consulting report)

_Compiled 5 November 2026 from sources retrieved in this research session. Classic studies are dated in the text. Tags used below: **UNVERIFIED** means the figure could not be checked against a primary source or the sources disagree; **secondary** means it was seen only in a source that cites the original; **self-reported** means it comes from the organisation running the programme. Where a figure came from a search-engine excerpt and not a full-page read, this is stated. Notes on relevance to IIT (private institute in Colombo, hot-humid climate, cooling-dominated loads, LKR 80m over 3 years, one developer plus final-year students) are in each "Inferences" block._

## 1. What energy/water savings have feedback, dashboards and sub-metering produced in universities and buildings, and how persistent are they?

### Takeaway
The meta-analyses agree that feedback typically saves about **5–12%**: Darby 2006 found 5–15% for direct feedback; ACEEE 2010 found 4–12%; Delmas et al. 2013 found an average of 7.4% across 156 trials. Real-time, granular feedback linked to goals works best. Campus results range widely: null in research buildings, about 6–10% for dashboards plus a campaign, and **32–55%** during a short dormitory competition with incentives (Petersen et al. 2007). Savings last only while the feedback continues. Effects decay by about **10–20% a year** once reports stop (Allcott & Rogers 2014). Social-comparison nudges can have no effect at all when occupants do not pay the energy bill (Myers & Souza 2020).

### Cited Findings

#### Classic dormitory study: Petersen et al. (2007), Oberlin College, USA
- Citation: Petersen, J.E., Shunturov, V., Janda, K., Platt, G. & Weinberger, K. (2007), "Dormitory residents reduce electricity consumption when exposed to real-time visual feedback and incentives", *International Journal of Sustainability in Higher Education* 8(1): 16–33, DOI 10.1108/14676370710717562 — [Emerald DOI](https://doi.org/10.1108/14676370710717562); [Oberlin Digital Commons record](https://digitalcommons.oberlin.edu/faculty_schol/1002)
- Design: a two-week "dorm energy competition" combining feedback, education and incentives. Two "high-resolution" dorms received real-time, web-based feedback. Twenty "low-resolution" dorms had their meters read manually, with data given weekly — [CBSM summary of Petersen et al. 2007](https://cbsm.com/articles/32594-dormitory-residents-reduce-electricity-consumption-when-exposed-to-real-time-visual-feedback-and-inc)
- Results: electricity use fell **32% overall**, saving **68,300 kWh, US$5,107 and 148,000 lb of CO2** during the competition. Water use fell only **3%**. The high-resolution (real-time) dorms cut electricity by **55%**, against **31%** for the low-resolution dorms — [CBSM summary](https://cbsm.com/articles/32594-dormitory-residents-reduce-electricity-consumption-when-exposed-to-real-time-visual-feedback-and-inc). (Note: the figure often quoted as "68,000 kWh" is 68,300 kWh in this summary.)
- Conflicting detail: EPA P3 grant-report summaries of the Oberlin project give a **13%** mean reduction for the 20 low-resolution dorms and say the winning dorm cut electricity by **56%**. This conflicts with the 31% in the journal abstract — [EPA NCER abstract 7816](https://cfpub.epa.gov/ncer_abstracts/index.cfm/fuseaction/display.abstractDetail/abstract_id/7816/report/F); [EPA NCER abstract 7327](https://cfpub.epa.gov/ncer_abstracts/index.cfm/fuseaction/display.abstractDetail/abstract_id/7327/report/F) (seen as search excerpts). The student newspaper reported savings of "68,500 kilowatts [sic]" and 20,500 gallons of water — [Oberlin Review, 26 May 2006](https://www2.oberlin.edu/stupub/ocreview/2006/05/26/commencement0506.pdf)
- Persistence: the only evidence is a post-competition survey. Students said they would keep the conservation practices and would check the real-time data even without a competition. The authors said they were studying persistence, but no result appears in the abstract — [CBSM summary](https://cbsm.com/articles/32594-dormitory-residents-reduce-electricity-consumption-when-exposed-to-real-time-visual-feedback-and-inc)

#### Reviews and meta-analyses (mostly households)
- **Darby (2006)**, *The effectiveness of feedback on energy consumption: a review for DEFRA of the literature on metering, billing and direct displays*, Environmental Change Institute, University of Oxford:
  - Direct feedback (from the meter or a display) usually saves **5–15%**. Indirect, bill-based feedback saves **0–10%**.
  - Sustained reduction needs instantaneous direct feedback together with frequent, accurate billing.
  - Sources: [Annex 66 listing of Darby 2006](https://annex66.org/?q=node/112); secondary citation in an [Ankara household feedback study (arXiv 1609.01309)](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1609.01309). Full report not retrieved; figures seen as excerpts and secondary citations.
- **Ehrhardt-Martinez, Donnelly & Laitner (2010)**, ACEEE Report E105, *Advanced Metering Initiatives and Residential Feedback Programs: A Meta-Review for Household Electricity-Saving Opportunities*:
  - Scope: 61 primary studies of 57 feedback programmes, 1974–2010.
  - Average household electricity savings were **4–12%** depending on the type of feedback: real-time feedback about **9.2%**, "real-time plus" (appliance-level) feedback about **12%**. US-only programmes saved 2–11%.
  - Savings persisted when the feedback persisted or sat in a supportive social environment.
  - Sources: [ACEEE E105 report page](https://www.aceee.org/research-report/e105); [E105 PDF](https://www.aceee.org/sites/default/files/pdfs/e105.pdf); [authors' slide deck, Sept 2010](https://www.aceee.org/sites/default/files/pdf/SKP-KEM-Energy-Feedback-Perspectives-Sep-14-2010.pdf). The ACEEE press release puts real-time feedback at about 9% per participating household — [PR Newswire, 2010](https://www.prnewswire.com/news-releases/aceee-study-finds-smart-meters-not-smart-enough-to-slash-residential-power-use-and-significantly-reduce-consumer-electric-bills-97403669.html)
- **Abrahamse, Steg, Vlek & Rothengatter (2005)**, "A review of intervention studies aimed at household energy conservation", *Journal of Environmental Psychology* 25(3): 273–291:
  - Feedback works, especially when it is frequent.
  - Information campaigns raise knowledge more reliably than they change behaviour.
  - Monetary rewards reduce use, but the effect fades quickly. How long effects last is often unclear.
  - Source: [CBSM summary](https://cbsm.com/articles/32615-a-review-of-intervention-studies-aimed-at-household-energy-conservation)
- **Karlin, Zinger & Ford (2015)**, "The effects of feedback on energy conservation: a meta-analysis", *Psychological Bulletin* 141(6): 1205–1227:
  - Scope: 42 studies, 1976–2010.
  - Feedback works overall (r = .071, p < .001), but effects vary widely (r from −.080 to .480).
  - Goal-setting with progress feedback and interactive real-time tools did best. Summaries on utility bills did worst. Comparison with neighbours was less effective than personal goal-setting.
  - Sources: [full-text PDF](https://faculty.sites.uci.edu/joannezinger/files/2019/08/Karlin-Zinger-Ford-2015-Psych-Bulletin-article.pdf); [APA press release, Sept 2015](https://www.apa.org/news/press/releases/2015/09/energy-use-feedback)
- **Delmas, Fischlein & Asensio (2013)**, "Information strategies and energy conservation behavior: a meta-analysis of experimental studies from 1975 to 2012", *Energy Policy* 61: 729–739:
  - Scope: 156 field trials. The average saving was a **7.4% reduction in electricity use**.
  - Individualised audits and consulting outperformed historical feedback and peer comparison.
  - Monetary (pecuniary) feedback and incentives were associated with a relative *increase* in use.
  - The measured effect shrinks as study rigour rises.
  - Sources: [UCLA PDF](https://www.ioes.ucla.edu/wp-content/uploads/2017/07/2013-Delmas-Fischlein-Asensio-EnergyPolicy.pdf); [EconPapers record](https://econpapers.repec.org/RePEc:eee:enepol:v:61:y:2013:i:c:p:729-739). The subject count is 525,479 on the [IOES publication page](https://ioes.ucla.edu/publication/information-strategies-and-energy-conservation-behavior-a-meta-analysis-of-experimental-studies-from-1975-to-2012) but 524,479 on the [UCLA dataset page](https://www.environment.ucla.edu/forms/energy-conservation-behavior-dataset).

#### Persistence of savings
- **Allcott & Rogers (2014)**, *American Economic Review* 104(10): 3003–3037 (randomised trials of Opower home energy reports):
  - The first reports cause cycles of "action and backsliding" that fade over time.
  - When reports stopped after about two years, savings persisted but decayed at **10–20% per year**.
  - Households were slow to tune out reports that continued.
  - Source: [NBER Working Paper w18492](https://www.nber.org/papers/18492)
- SAVES, the Student Switch Off campaign in five European countries, delivered consistent savings of about **7% over two academic years** — [Bull et al. (2018), "Competing priorities: lessons in engaging students to achieve energy savings in universities", IJSHE 19(7): 1220–1238](https://www.emerald.com/ijshe/article/19/7/1220/154732/Competing-priorities-lessons-in-engaging-students)
- ACEEE: savings persist when the feedback persists — [ACEEE E105](https://www.aceee.org/research-report/e105). Darby: feedback must continue to sustain reductions — [Annex 66 listing](https://annex66.org/?q=node/112)

#### When occupants do not pay the bill (important for students)
- **Myers & Souza (2020)**, "Social comparison nudges without monetary incentives: Evidence from home energy reports", *Journal of Environmental Economics and Management* 101: 102315:
  - This was a randomised trial in a university residence where tenants do not pay energy bills.
  - The reports produced almost no change in heating demand; the estimates rule out thermostat changes larger than 0.36°F.
  - Students did respond to simpler nudges, such as reminders to save energy while away on vacation.
  - The authors conclude that behavioural channels may not motivate conservation without a direct monetary incentive.
  - Sources: [E2e Working Paper 041 (2018 version)](https://sustainability.illinois.edu/wp-content/uploads/2018/10/WP041.pdf); [Illinois Experts record](https://experts.illinois.edu/en/publications/social-comparison-nudges-without-monetary-incentives-evidence-fro/)
- Counterpoint: Delmas & Lessem (2014) found that publicly displaying the most and least efficient residents in university residence halls significantly reduced electricity use, as summarised in a later paper — [MPRA paper 102934](https://mpra.ub.uni-muenchen.de/102934/1/MPRA_paper_102934.pdf). The size of the effect was not retrieved.

#### Campus dashboards and building-level feedback (2010s–2020s)
- University of Illinois Energy Sense study: dashboards in four buildings on four community-college campuses, plus a six-week behaviour campaign, saved about **7–10% of electricity and about 50% of natural gas** — [UIUC Energy Sense](https://energysense.uillinois.edu/energy-dashboards/)
- Virginia Tech: comparative electricity feedback in campus research buildings produced reductions of **under 1%** against a control group, which was not statistically significant — [VTechWorks thesis](https://vtechworks.lib.vt.edu/handle/10919/23128)
- A university-wide comparative feedback campaign: participating buildings cut electricity per square foot by **6.5%**, while non-participating buildings rose by **2.4%** — ["Using comparative feedback to influence workplace energy conservation: a case study of a university campaign" (*Environment and Behavior*)](https://metatoc.com/papers/29904-using-comparative-feedback-to-influence-workplace-energy-conservation-a-case-study-of-a-university-campaign) (seen as a search excerpt; authors not retrieved)
- Plug-load dashboards produced statistically significant reductions in both a government office (NASA Ames) and university settings — [arXiv 2004.06633](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2004.06633). Percentages not retrieved.
- Vendor-reported: Washington & Lee University (USA) cut energy use by more than 30% and carbon emissions by 34% using Atrius BuildingOS — [Atrius case study](https://www.casestudies.com/company/atrius/case-study/how-washington-lee-built-a-culture-of-sustainability-while-centralizing-and-automating-energy-data). This is marketing material, not peer-reviewed.

#### Metering and monitoring & targeting at organisation level
- Carbon Trust, via secondary sources:
  - Monitoring and targeting (M&T) typically saves about 5% of energy costs, based on a study of more than 1,000 small businesses — [Wikipedia: Energy monitoring and targeting](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Energy_monitoring_and_targeting)
  - In a Carbon Trust field trial, organisations that adopted advanced metering cut carbon emissions by an average of **12%** and saved **5%** financially — [MODBS / Trend Control Systems article](https://modbs.co.uk/news/fullstory.php/aid/12936/Metering_energy_leads__to_saving_energy.html)
  - **Secondary**: the primary Carbon Trust publication was not retrieved.

#### Tropical, cooling-dominated comparator: Universiti Teknologi Malaysia (UTM)
- Results of UTM's Sustainable Energy Management Program, self-reported. The figures differ slightly between UTM sources:
  - **2010–2012 against a 2009 benchmark:** electricity use fell by 14,542,426 kWh (**−24.3%**), the bill fell by RM 4,434,767 (**−16.8%**) and the energy efficiency index fell by 10.78%.
  - **2010–2013 cumulative:** 21 million kWh and RM 6.6 million saved.
  - **2023:** about 4.5 million kWh and about RM 2.5 million saved.
  - Sources: [UTM Sustainable Energy Management page](https://sustainable.utm.my/sustainable-energy-management/); [UTM News](https://news.utm.my/?p=10237); [UTM energy-efficiency presentation hosted by Malaysia's Energy Commission](https://oasapp.st.gov.my/my/general/add_counter/255/download/read_count). These figures were seen as search excerpts, and which figure comes from which of the three sources is approximate.

### Inferences
- **Planning range for IIT:**
  - Dashboards plus engagement: assume 5–10% in teaching and office space.
  - Technical energy management (air-conditioning schedules, set-points, fixing faults found through sub-metering): 15–25% is realistic, as UTM's 24% kWh cut in a similar climate shows.
  - Reductions above 30% are realistic only during short, incentivised competitions, and should not be treated as a baseline expectation.
- IIT students do not pay the electricity bill, so dashboards built only on social comparison may underperform (Myers & Souza). Effort and budget should go mainly to automated controls and loads managed by facilities, such as AC scheduling, set-points and out-of-hours shutdown. Visible competitions and recognition (Petersen; SAVES) can be added on top.
- In Colombo's hot-humid climate, air-conditioning is very likely IIT's dominant electrical load. This is an inference, as no IIT load data was available. If so, granular sub-metering of AC and chillers will matter more than lighting or plug-load feedback.
- Persistence requires *continuous* feedback. An always-on dashboard with an annual campaign refresh is better than a one-off campaign. If campaigns stop, assume savings decay by 10–20% a year (Allcott & Rogers).
- Feedback has a much smaller effect on water use (only 3% at Oberlin). Water metering is better justified by leak detection and baseline reporting (GreenMetric's Water criterion) than by behaviour-change claims.
- Petersen's 55%-versus-31% contrast compares only two high-resolution dorms with twenty others, so it should be presented as indicative, not definitive.

### Gaps
- The full text of Darby (2006) was not retrieved, so its numbers come from excerpts and secondary citations.
- The ACEEE (2010) percentages for the lower feedback tiers (for example enhanced billing at about 3.8%) were not confirmed. Only 6.8%, 8.4%, 9.2% and 12.0% appeared in the retrieved excerpts.
- The commonly cited "38 studies" count for Abrahamse et al. (2005) was not verified.
- No peer-reviewed, multi-year persistence study specific to campus dashboards was found.
- The size of the Delmas & Lessem (2014) effect was not retrieved.
- No campus feedback trial from South Asia, or from a tropical setting, was found apart from UTM's programme data.
- Fischer (2008) and Zangheri et al. (2019) were not checked.

## 2. What results have engagement programmes and gamified challenges reported (Student Switch Off, Green Impact, campus energy competitions)?

### Takeaway
Student campaigns that run over several years in several countries deliver roughly **6–7%** energy savings in residences: SAVES/Student Switch Off reported about 7% over two years, and SAVES 2 reported 6.78%. Single-campus and short competitions report bigger short-term cuts: Worcester 16% (2023–24) and DePauw 25.8% (2010). The US Campus Conservation Nationals saved about 0.5–2 GWh per edition across 40–125 schools. Green Impact reports very large engagement numbers, but its carbon figures are mostly self-reported. None of these programmes has been evaluated by a randomised trial.

### Cited Findings
- **SAVES, first phase (Student Switch Off in five European countries, academic years 2014–15 and 2015–16):** consistent savings of about **7%** over two years across a large number of dorms — [Bull et al. 2018, IJSHE 19(7)](https://www.emerald.com/ijshe/article/19/7/1220/154732/Competing-priorities-lessons-in-engaging-students). The method compared each dorm's 2014–15 electricity use with its use before the campaign, so it is a before-and-after design, not a randomised one — [Sustainability Exchange: SAVES end of first year results](https://sustainabilityexchange.ac.uk/news/saves_project_end_of_first_year_results). Related paper: [eceee 2017, "Switching off? Challenges in engaging students in energy efficiency"](https://www.eceee.org/library/conference_proceedings/eceee_Summer_Studies/2017/9-consumption-and-behaviour/switching-off-challenges-in-engaging-students-in-energy-efficiency-findings-from-an-eu-wide-energy-saving-project/)
- **SAVES 2 (EU Horizon 2020 grant 754203):**
  - Coverage: 14 universities in 7 countries.
  - Engagement: **45,592 students** signed up (37% of the student population), including **606** student ambassadors.
  - Result: an overall saving of **6.78%** against baseline.
  - Method: consumption models built from baseline-period data under the International Measurement and Verification framework (IPMVP). The project also noted that more research is needed on what dashboards contribute to behaviour-change savings.
  - Sources: [CORDIS SAVES 2 reporting](https://cordis.europa.eu/project/id/754203/reporting); [CORDIS results](https://cordis.europa.eu/project/id/754203/results)
- **Student Switch Off UK, 2023–24:**
  - Nationally, **983,017 kWh** of electricity was saved, equal to about **227 t of CO2**. This total comes from a single university's campaign report, not from an official SOS-UK release.
  - At the University of Worcester, energy use across residences fell **16%**, saving about **6 t of CO2**.
  - Source: [University of Worcester SSO Campaign Report 2023–24](https://www.worc.ac.uk/documents/university-of-worcester-student-switch-off-campaign-report.pdf)
- Imperial College London 2023–24: 494 students engaged, 15.6% of residents — [Imperial 2023–24 SSO Campaign Report](https://imperial.ac.uk/media/imperial-college/about/sustainability/2023-24-ICL-Campaign-Report.pdf)
- Self-reported change in behaviour: "Students in residences report 68% increase in energy saving behaviours" (headline) — [SOS-UK post](https://sustainability.nus.org.uk/post/students-in-residences-report-68-increase-in-energy-saving-behaviours)
- SOS-UK cumulative claim: more than 1.1 million students reached in halls over 12 years, and "over 25 MWh" saved, equated to 15,000 t CO2. The units are internally inconsistent (25 MWh cannot equal 15,000 t CO2; GWh is probably meant), and the page blocked automated access — [SOS-UK post](https://sos-uk.org/post/encouraging-sustainable-living-in-halls-of-residence) — **UNVERIFIED**
- Student Switch Off is listed as an Ashden Award winner — [Ashden: Student Switch Off](https://ashden.org/awards/winners/student-switch-off/)
- **Green Impact (SOS-UK), all self-reported:**
  - **Engagement totals:** the 2024–25 university introduction reports **44,999 teams, 376,371 people reached, 7,718 students trained and 559,901 actions completed**. These look like cumulative totals since the programme began, not annual ones — [Green Impact 2024–25 university introduction](https://greenimpact.nus.org.uk/wp-content/uploads/2024/02/2024-25-Green-Impact-university-introduction.pdf)
  - **Participants and fees (2025–26):** more than 50 organisations take part. The university fee is **£9,900 + VAT** in the first year (up to 1,500 FTE staff) and £9,500 + VAT in later years — [Green Impact 2025–26 university introduction](https://greenimpact.nus.org.uk/wp-content/uploads/2025/07/2025-26-Green-Impact-university-introduction.pdf); [2025–26 university brochure](https://greenimpact.nus.org.uk/wp-content/uploads/2025/09/2025-26-Green-Impact-university-brochure.pdf)
- University of York Green Impact (2022): teams completed 897 actions, saving about **90 t of CO2** and about **£16,531**, and raised £3,500 for charity — [University of York staff news, 2022](https://www.york.ac.uk/staff/news/2022/departments-green-impact/)
- How Green Impact works: an online toolkit of 42 actions plus a projects section (LSE 2021–22) — [LSE Green Impact Impact Report 2022](https://info.lse.ac.uk/staff/divisions/estates-division/sustainable-lse/assets/documents/get-involved/green-impact/2022-LSE-Green-Impact-Impact-Report.pdf). Trained student auditors check that actions were completed, and a team project can earn up to 50 points — [University of Salford: Green Impact](https://salford.ac.uk/environmental-sustainability/green-impact)
- **Campus Conservation Nationals (North American inter-campus competition):**
  - **2010:** 40 schools cut electricity by **508,694 kWh** (about US$50,209). DePauw University averaged a **25.8%** reduction across its halls — [FMLink, 2010](https://www.fmlink.com/120000-students-save-500000-kwh-of-electricity-in-campus-conservation-contest/)
  - **2012:** about 200,000 students at about 100 schools saved **1,739,046 kWh** (1.74 GWh), beating the 1 GWh goal — [PR Newswire, 2012](https://prnewswire.com/news-releases/200000-students-at-100-colleges-and-universities-save-174-gigawatt-hours-of-electricity-during-campus-conservation-nationals-2012-149641725.html). Bowling Green State University took first place with **237,239 kWh** — [BGSU](https://www.bgsu.edu/campus-sustainability/energy/campus-conservation-nationals)
  - **Previous year, as reported in a 2014 campus post:** more than 2 million kWh and 1.5 million gallons of water saved — [Oregon State "Ecologue", April 2014](https://blogs.oregonstate.edu/ecologue/2014/04/06/campus-conservation-nationals-2014-results/)
  - **2015:** more than 343,000 students and staff at 125 schools, with about **6 GWh** saved cumulatively over the first five years — [ES Magazine](https://www.esmagazine.com/articles/97162-universities-compete-to-reduce-energy-waste?v=preview). This was attributed to the outlet from search excerpts and should be verified.

### Inferences
- Student Switch Off is built around residence halls, and it is unclear whether IIT has student residences. A model better suited to IIT would be Green Impact-style team toolkits for departments and labs, combined with competitions between floors or faculties scored on sub-meter data.
- Green Impact's licence (about £9,900 a year, UK pricing) and SAVES's measurement method (IPMVP baselines) could be copied in-house cheaply. One developer plus final-year students could build:
  - an action toolkit;
  - student auditing;
  - a leaderboard;
  - weather-normalised baselines using cooling degree-days for Colombo.
- A reasonable sustained target is 5–7% savings in the targeted buildings if the campaign runs every year. Short competitions will produce spikes that should not be booked as permanent savings.
- The money saved per tonne of CO2 will differ in Sri Lanka from the UK. Grid carbon intensity and tariffs differ (see Q7), so savings should be reported in kWh and LKR as well as tCO2e.

### Gaps
- No randomised controlled trial of Student Switch Off was found. The SAVES figures are before-and-after comparisons against baselines.
- Official SOS-UK national impact totals for 2023–24 and 2024–25 were not verified (the SOS-UK site blocked automated access).
- No independent evaluation of Green Impact's carbon savings across the sector was found.
- No Campus Conservation Nationals results after 2015 were found.
- No evaluated gamified energy or waste challenge in a South Asian university was found.

## 3. What savings have university reuse platforms (e.g. Warp It, textbook and equipment swaps) reported?

### Takeaway
UK universities using the Warp It reuse portal self-report savings ranging from tens of thousands to hundreds of thousands of pounds a year, plus tens to hundreds of tonnes of CO2e. Liverpool reported **£300,442 and 136.9 tCO2e** in its first 12 months, and Kent reported **£794,281 and 381 tCO2e** cumulatively. The money comes mainly from purchases avoided, plus disposal costs avoided. All figures come from the platform's calculators, and no peer-reviewed evaluation was found.

### Cited Findings
- **University of Liverpool:** Warp It was launched in Sustainability Week 2025. In its first 12 months it delivered **£300,442** in savings and prevented **36.6 t of waste** and **136.9 tCO2e**. The target is more than £1 million in savings by 2031 — [University of Liverpool sustainability news](https://www.liverpool.ac.uk/about/sustainability/news/stories/title,1545664,en.php)
- **University of Nottingham:** **80 t of waste** kept out of landfill and **192 t of CO2** saved (no cost figure or date given) — [Nottingham: Warp It](https://www.nottingham.ac.uk/sustainability/waste/warpit.aspx)
- **Loughborough University (2025 calendar year):** **£24,790** saved, **1.9 t** of waste and **12,845 kg of CO2** avoided, from 42 claims — [Loughborough internal news, Feb 2026](https://lboro.ac.uk/internal/news/2026/february/how-warpit-is-reducing-waste-across-campus/)
- **University of Kent (cumulative, undated):** **£794,281** in costs saved, **129 t** of waste diverted and **381 t of CO2** saved — [Kent recycling blog](https://blogs.kent.ac.uk/recycle/?p=291)
- **University of Birmingham (Feb 2020):** **32 t of CO2** and **£74,000** in disposal and related costs saved — [University of Birmingham news, 2020](https://www.birmingham.ac.uk/news-archive/2020/university-saves-32-tonnes-of-co2-through-re-use-scheme)
- **Sector-wide (older):** Warp It said it had saved higher-education institutions **£3m**, and all customers **£8m** in avoided procurement, since it was founded in 2011 — [edie](https://www.edie.net/news/related_stories.asp?id=34434). The article is undated (probably around 2017) and these are company claims.
- Warp It is also used outside the UK, for example at University College Dublin — [UCD Work Smarter Together](https://worksmartertogether.ucd.ie/ucd-warp-it-waste-action-reuse-portal/)
- University of Otago (New Zealand) "e-Rescue" for IT: more than **1,240 desktops and laptops** rescued and reused in the community through an on-campus thrift shop and direct provision to vulnerable students — [University of Otago news](https://www.otago.ac.nz/news/newsroom/innovative-e-rescue-approach-brings-social,-environmental-benefits)

### Inferences
- Savings scale with institution size and with how many items people claim. Loughborough's 42 claims produced about £25k, while Liverpool's first year produced about £300k. IIT is smaller than these universities, so it should expect smaller ongoing savings. The **move to the Ratmalana campus is a one-off peak** in surplus furniture and equipment, so a reuse marketplace launched before the move can capture a disproportionate share of purchases avoided.
- With one developer and final-year students, IIT can build a simple internal marketplace instead of licensing a platform:
  - item listings, claims and logged item weight and category;
  - CO2e calculated from published emission factors;
  - a direct feed into the waste and reuse evidence that GreenMetric's Waste criterion (18% weight, see Q6) and THE's SDG 12 evidence ask for.
- Reported "savings" should separate cash actually avoided (purchases not made, disposal fees avoided) from notional replacement value. Kent's and Liverpool's figures most likely use replacement-cost calculators, but the method was not reviewed.

### Gaps
- No peer-reviewed or independent evaluation of Warp It or any other university reuse platform was found. The CO2 and cost calculation methods were not reviewed.

## 4. IT asset lifecycle: how much of a laptop's or desktop's footprint comes from manufacturing, what does extending device life deliver, and why do certified e-waste handling and chain of custody matter?

### Takeaway
Manufacturing accounts for most of a laptop's lifecycle carbon:
- **about 56–78%** in most manufacturer reports and academic life-cycle assessments (one 2012 dataset gives as low as 35%);
- **about 76–77%** for Apple MacBook Air models.

Extending device life is therefore the biggest single lever. A new, more efficient notebook needs **33–89 years** of energy savings to offset its production emissions (Öko-Institut 2012). Giving every EU notebook one extra year of life would save about **1.6 Mt CO2 a year** (EEB 2019).

Disposal outside controlled channels carries environmental and data risks. About **one-third** of GPS-tracked US e-waste was exported, mostly to Hong Kong (Basel Action Network 2016). Morgan Stanley paid a **US$35m** SEC penalty (2022) after decommissioned drives were resold with customer data on them. Both cases support using certified recyclers (R2v3, e-Stewards, EN 50625/WEEELABEX) with documented chain of custody and certificates of data sanitisation.

#### Why chain of custody matters: evidence
- **Basel Action Network (BAN) GPS-tracker study (May 2016):**
  - About **one-third** of roughly 200–205 tracked electronic items delivered to US recycling channels were exported, mostly to Hong Kong.
  - **39%** of the tracked items left with recyclers were exported. At Goodwill stores, **7 of 46 (15%)** were exported.
  - Sources: [The Intercept, 10 May 2016](https://theintercept.com/2016/05/10/gps-tracking-devices-catch-major-u-s-recyclers-in-improper-e-waste-exports); [OPB "The Circuit"](https://www.opb.org/news/series/circuit/track-electronic-waste-behind-scenes-reporting)
- **BAN "Scam Recycling: e-Dumping on Asia by US Recyclers" (Sept 2016):** **40% of 152** deliveries to US e-scrap recyclers were exported, and BAN judged **96%** of those exports likely illegal — [Resource Recycling, 15 Sept 2016](https://resource-recycling.com/e-scrap/2016/09/15/ban-names-companies-involved-in-exporting/?noamp=mobile). A separate 2018 BAN tracker study found similar exports from Canada — [BAN, Oct 2018](https://www.ban.org/news-new/2018/10/10/gps-trackers-reveal-more-canadian-e-waste-exported-to-developing-countries)
- **Data-security failure: Morgan Stanley Smith Barney paid a US$35m SEC penalty (Sept 2022):**
  - The firm hired a moving and storage company with no data-destruction experience and failed to monitor its work.
  - Thousands of devices, some holding customer personal data, were resold and ended up on an internet auction site with the data still on them.
  - About 15 million customers were affected. 42 servers were missing, and encryption had not been switched on.
  - Sources: [The Record](https://therecord.media/morgan-stanley-to-pay-35-million-fee-for-astonishing-customer-data-disposal-practices/); [TechSpot](https://www.techspot.com/news/96058-sec-fines-morgan-stanley-35-million-after-exposing.html)

#### Certification standards (mostly from consultant or vendor summaries)
- **R2v3 (SERI):**
  - Requires data sanitisation aligned with NIST SP 800-88 — [BitRaser: R2v3 Core 7](https://www.bitraser.com/article/r2v3-core-7-data-security-requirements)
  - Requires certified vendors to check that downstream partners also meet the standard — [Securis](https://securis.com/?p=17450)
  - Each facility must be certified individually, and over 900 facilities are certified worldwide — [NSYS Group](https://nsysgroup.com/blog/e-stewards-vs-r2-what-certification-to-choose/)
- **e-Stewards (Basel Action Network):**
  - Explicitly prohibits exporting hazardous e-waste to non-OECD countries — [ERI](https://eridirect.com/blog/2024/11/understanding-e-stewards-and-r2-certifications/)
  - About 100 certified facilities worldwide — [NSYS Group](https://nsysgroup.com/blog/e-stewards-vs-r2-what-certification-to-choose/)
  - Data destruction is often evidenced through the separate NAID AAA certification — [Beyond Surplus](https://www.beyondsurplus.com/?p=7496)
- **EN 50625 series (CENELEC, EU) and WEEELABEX:**
  - The standards set treatment and depollution requirements for waste electrical and electronic equipment (WEEE), with target values for removing batteries and capacitors and limit values for hazardous substances at the end of treatment — [CEN-CENELEC WEEE brochure](https://www.cencenelec.eu/media/CEN-CENELEC/AreasOfWork/CEN-CENELEC_Topics/Environment%20and%20Sustainability/Quicklinks%20General/Documentation%20and%20Materials/weee-brochure.pdf)
  - The WEEE Forum argues that EN 50625 should be applied as one integrated, legally binding set. The WEEELABEX Office is an accredited organisation that qualifies auditors and operators — [WEEE Forum statement](https://weee-forum.org/?p=1874)

### Inferences
- **Worked example (illustrative arithmetic only):** assume manufacturing, transport and end-of-life are fixed and make up 75% of a laptop's footprint over a 4-year life (consistent with Apple and Dell figures of 68–78%).
  - Extending life to **5 years** cuts the footprint per year of service by about **15%**.
  - Extending to **6 years** cuts it by about **25%**.
  - With a 62% manufacturing share (Dell Latitude 5455), the cuts are about 12% and 21%.
- **Purchasing:** moving the refresh cycle from 4 to 5 years cuts annual device purchases by **20%**; from 4 to 6 years, by **33%**; from 3 to 5 years, by **40%**. For a computing-heavy institute with large lab fleets, this is probably IIT's largest IT-related carbon *and* cost lever. Upgrading RAM and SSDs, replacing batteries and redeploying devices from high-spec labs to admin use are the practical routes.
- **Effect of Sri Lanka's grid:** Sri Lanka's average grid intensity (about 0.33–0.34 kg/kWh in recent years; see Q7) is not unusually high, so the use phase does not change the conclusion. Manufacturing remains the dominant share for laptops used in Colombo.
- **Disposal partners and data protection:**
  - Sri Lanka's formal e-waste system handles only a small fraction of the estimated arisings (3,000 t a year against 25,000–175,000 t), and only 12–16 collectors are licensed.
  - IIT should therefore use only CEA-licensed collectors and require certificates of data sanitisation (for example to NIST SP 800-88) and downstream disclosure. Where available, it should prefer R2v3 or e-Stewards processors, or EN 50625-compliant ones.
  - The Morgan Stanley case shows that the data-protection exposure can exceed the hardware's value. Sri Lanka's Personal Data Protection Act (2022) is relevant but was not checked in this session.
- **Asset register as the enabler:** a digital asset register showing device, owner, age, warranty, condition and final disposition is what makes life extension, internal redeployment and donation measurable. It can also supply Scope 3 purchased-goods data for reporting. Final-year students could build it.

### Gaps
- No specific manufacturing share was retrieved for HP or Lenovo laptops, and the Apple M3 lifecycle breakdown was not retrieved.
- No university-specific study quantifying the money or carbon saved by extending refresh cycles was found. The figures above are arithmetic, not observed outcomes.
- The university reuse and donation programmes found report device counts, not CO2.
- WEEELABEX audit and depollution criteria were not retrieved directly.
- No information was found on whether any Sri Lankan e-waste processor holds R2, e-Stewards or EN 50625 certification.
- The R2 and e-Stewards facility counts and requirement summaries come from consultant or vendor blogs, not from the SERI or BAN standard texts.

## 5. Which university "living labs" use operational data for teaching and research, and with what outcomes (Monash, UBC, Manchester, Leuphana, others)?

### Takeaway
The best-documented living labs combine large capital programmes with research partnerships:
- **Monash** cut Scope 1+2 emissions by **57% below its 2015 baseline by 2023**, using a microgrid backed by ARENA (Australia's renewable energy agency) as a test-bed.
- At **UBC**, a single bioenergy research and demonstration plant cut campus GHG emissions by about 12–14%, and campus-wide emissions fell 34–38% from 2007.
- **Leuphana** achieved its first climate-neutral energy balance in **2014**.
- **Manchester's** living lab is mainly a teaching and research brokerage with more than 150 projects, but its impact is only anecdotal.
- In the tropics, **NTU Singapore's** EcoCampus is a direct comparator for IIT. It targeted 35% cuts in energy, water and waste intensity, but the published savings are simulated, not measured.

### Inferences
- IIT's living-lab advantage is low-cost and suited to its strengths. Its students study computing, data science and software engineering, and the new Ratmalana campus already has smart meters. This fits a **data living lab**, using live meter data for final-year projects on dashboards, anomaly detection, AC optimisation and forecasting, better than a capital-heavy energy living lab like Monash's microgrid or UBC's bioenergy plant.
- Manchester shows that a living lab can scale at low cost as a project brokerage, but it also shows the weakness: impact is not measured. IIT should build impact tracking into each project from the start, recording the baseline metric, the intervention, the measured change and whether the change was adopted by operations.
- NTU's EcoCampus is the most climate-relevant model for IIT. Its simulated 31% saving from envelope, lighting, plug-load and chiller measures indicates the scale of potential in hot-humid buildings. IIT should not quote it as an achieved result.

### Gaps

- UBC's latest Climate Action Plan 2030 progress figures were not retrieved, and the UBC numbers above vary by source and year.
- No audited outcome data for Manchester's Living Lab was found.
- No information was retrieved on Leuphana's current emissions figures or how much offsetting it uses.

## 6. Which universities use a consolidated sustainability data platform to set baselines, set targets and report (GRI, UI GreenMetric, THE, national returns), and is there evidence that measurement enabled savings?

### Takeaway
Leading universities report against a fixed baseline using consolidated data:
- **Monash** reports against 2015 and is 57% down.
- **Edinburgh** met its 2007/08 baseline targets, cutting carbon intensity per £m of turnover from 156 to 65.5.
- In the UK, the **HESA Estates Management Record** collects sector energy, carbon, water, waste and transport data. 138 providers submitted Scope 1+2 data for 2022/23 (1.4 MtCO2; 7.0 TWh).

The rankings now demand this kind of data at scale. **THE Impact Rankings 2025** ranked a record 2,526 universities from 130 countries. **UI GreenMetric** scores six criteria, with energy and climate carrying the most weight.

Direct evidence that measurement *by itself* causes savings is modest: about 5% (cost) to 12% (carbon) in Carbon Trust metering trials. Measurement mainly enables savings by making savings verifiable and by targeting technical fixes.

### Cited Findings
- **HESA Estates Management Record (EMR), UK:**
  - Coverage: annual open data on buildings and grounds, energy, renewable generation, water, emissions, waste, recycling, transport and environmental management — [HESA: Estates Management open data](https://www.hesa.ac.uk/node/137497)
  - Status: the return is **statutory in Wales and optional in England, Scotland and Northern Ireland**. HESA is now part of Jisc.
  - 2022/23 data: **138 providers** submitted Scope 1 and 2 data, totalling **1.4 MtCO2**, with energy use of about **7.0 TWh** and on-site renewable generation of **117 GWh (2%)** — [HESA: environmental information](https://hesa.ac.uk/node/142516); [HESA: 2021/22](https://www.hesa.ac.uk/node/141280)

  - The university says it met both its targets: returning absolute emissions to 2007/08 baseline levels, and cutting emissions per £m of turnover by 50%. - **Sri Lankan sector context:** many Sri Lankan universities have set up sustainability committees and begun applying to the THE Impact Rankings and UI GreenMetric. Reported gaps include the lack of designated sustainability managers or officers — [Daily Mirror (Sri Lanka): "Greener campuses, higher rankings: Priority for SL Unis"](https://www.dailymirror.lk/news-features/Greener-campuses-higher-rankings-Priority-for-SL-Unis/131-310302)

### Inferences
- IIT currently has no consolidated data or baseline, so **the first deliverable should be a single data model** covering:
  - energy by meter, building and end-use;
  - water;
  - waste and reuse;
  - IT assets;
  - transport;
  - engagement.

  It should mirror UI GreenMetric's six criteria and the evidence the THE Impact Rankings ask for on SDG 7 (energy), SDG 12 (consumption and production) and SDG 13 (climate). The HESA EMR field list is a ready-made reference schema.
- **Baseline:** fix a baseline year at the Ratmalana move (for example the first 12 months of operation) and normalise by floor area, full-time-equivalent students and cooling degree-days. Without this, IIT cannot credibly claim the "leading sustainable technology campus by 2030" position.
- **What the data delivers:** consolidated data mainly delivers *verifiable* savings and ranking eligibility. Measurement alone is worth only about 5–12% (Carbon Trust), and the larger savings come from actions the data points to (UTM, Monash).
- **Scale of the rankings task:** the THE Impact Rankings are now majority-Asian (52%), and GreenMetric has more than 1,500 participants, so being ranked alone will not differentiate IIT. Rank position depends on documented, data-backed evidence.

### Gaps

### Inferences
- **The competitive bar for IIT's ambition** of becoming "Sri Lanka's leading sustainable technology campus" is set by state universities at around rank 186–190 worldwide in GreenMetric (scores about 8,200/10,000), and by NSBM's green-campus branding. IIT's credible point of difference is not "greenest campus". It is **"most data-driven, digitally enabled sustainability campus"**: real-time meter data, an open dashboard, a student living lab and IT circularity, all of which match its computing focus.
- **Choice of emission factor:** the value matters a lot. The 2017 combined margin (0.81) is more than twice the recent average intensity (0.33–0.34). IIT should state its chosen factor and where it comes from in its baseline, and should probably use the latest official SLSEA factor once obtained.
- **Effect of tariff volatility:** swings of ±15–20% within a single year change payback periods. The business case should be modelled in kWh with a tariff sensitivity range, not at a single LKR rate.
- **Value for money:** on its own figures, NSBM's 50 kW solar pilot covered only 3–5% of monthly electricity. This suggests that, for IIT's budget, metering, controls, AC optimisation and device-life extension give more savings per rupee than small-scale PV. This is an inference, and IIT's roof area and solar options were not assessed.
